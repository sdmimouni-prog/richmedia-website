## Two complementary logics

SEO remains essential for demand capture on Google. GEO adds a new requirement: clear, structured answers that AI engines can understand and cite.

## What changes for brands

Content must answer quickly, prove expertise, connect pages together and avoid vague wording. Precision becomes a visibility lever.

## Build a durable base

A strong strategy combines architecture, internal linking, expert pages, structured data, useful FAQs and updated content.

## What GEO adds to SEO

GEO does not replace SEO. It adds a readability layer for conversational engines and generated answers. A page needs to clearly state what it knows, why it is reliable and how it connects to the rest of the website.

For a brand, this changes content writing. Vague claims, generic promises and overly decorative pages become less useful. Winning pages answer fast, structure use cases, show examples, explain decision criteria and offer a logical next step.

## Build pages that are easy to cite

A GEO-ready page must be easy to split into answers. This requires explicit headings, short paragraphs, tables, FAQs and clear definitions. AI engines look for reliable elements they can summarize without losing meaning.

Editorial work also needs to connect topics. A SEO/GEO page should link to strategy, content, data, acquisition, sector and case study pages. Internal linking helps Google, AI engines and users understand the full scope of expertise.

## Measure visibility differently

In 2026, classic rankings are no longer enough. Teams need to track impressions, organic traffic, cited pages, long-tail queries, assisted conversions and how content appears in AI-generated answers.

A strong SEO/GEO strategy remains operational: clear architecture, useful content, concrete proof, technical optimization and regular monitoring. The difference is that each page must now convince users, Google and answer engines at the same time.
